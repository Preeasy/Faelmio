Most Amazon toy listings do not get suspended for a bad product. They get suspended for a document that does not match the ASIN, a warning label in the wrong place, or a category restriction nobody checked before the container shipped.

This is a working checklist, in the order the work actually happens. It complements the document-level detail in [what gets a CPC rejected](/blog/cpc-certificate-amazon-toys/) — here the focus is everything around the certificate that a reviewer also checks.

Requirements change. Confirm the current rules with a CPSC-accepted lab and your Amazon account manager before committing to a print run.

## Stage 1 — before you order

**1. Confirm the category is open to you.** Toys & Games is a gated category in many marketplaces. Check whether you need approval, and whether approval requires documents up front. Finding out after production is a five-figure mistake.

**2. Check the product against recall history.** Search the CPSC recall database and the EU Safety Gate for the product type. Fidgets, magnetic building sets, water beads and button-cell novelties have long recall histories. If your item resembles a recalled product, expect scrutiny.

**3. Confirm restricted sub-categories.** High-powered magnets are regulated in the US; water beads and some projectile toys face marketplace restrictions; laser toys, chemistry sets and toy guns have their own rules. Amazon applies its own policy on top of the law, and it is stricter than the law.

**4. Fix the age grade before tooling.** The age grade drives the test protocol, the warning text, the small-parts assessment and the listing attributes. An age grade changed after testing means retesting.

**5. Fix the importer identity.** In the US, the CPC must name the importer of record. In the EU, the listing and label must name the EU responsible person. Decide these parties before the labels are printed — both appear on packaging.

## Stage 2 — testing and documents

**6. US: CPC built from a CPSC-accepted lab report.** The lab report is not the certificate; the importer issues the CPC. It must reference the model/ASIN, the age grade, the rules tested (ASTM F963 and CPSIA at minimum), and the lab's acceptance number. See the [CPC guide](/blog/cpc-certificate-amazon-toys/) for the five rejection reasons.

**7. US: CPSIA tracking label.** Children's products need a permanent mark on the product and its packaging showing manufacturer or private labeler name, location and date of production, and a batch or cohort identifier. A carton barcode alone is not a tracking label. This is one of the most common review failures because factories often print it as an afterthought, or not at all.

**8. EU: CE marking, DoC and test reports.** EN 71-1/2/3 for the toy, plus EN 62115 for anything electrical, plus EMC or radio approval where applicable. The Declaration of Conformity must name the product and the standards applied.

**9. EU: GPSR listing fields.** Since 13 December 2024 the marketplace listing must show the manufacturer's name and address, the EU responsible person's name, postal address and email, and product safety information. Amazon enforces this in the listing back end. The full requirement set is in the [GPSR guide](/blog/eu-gpsr-toy-sellers/).

**10. Battery documents, if the toy contains a battery.** UN38.3 report, MSDS and test summary for the cell or pack, plus the applicable FCC or RED approval for anything that transmits. Amazon asks for these for FBA inbound lithium shipments. Details are in the [RC and electronic toy guide](/blog/rc-electronic-toys-compliance/).

**11. Photograph the actual label.** Reviewers want to see the warning text, the tracking label or CE mark, the importer address and the age grade as printed on the retail unit. A mock-up from the design file is not proof that production matches.

## Stage 3 — the warning text

Warning wording is where small errors cost the most, because it is printed.

| Situation | Required wording (US) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Small parts, under 3 | "WARNING: CHOKING HAZARD — Small parts. Not for children under 3 yrs." | Exact wording and layout; do not paraphrase |
| Small ball | "WARNING: CHOKING HAZARD — This toy is a small ball. Not for children under 3 yrs." | Ball-specific variant |
| Balloon | "WARNING: CHOKING HAZARD — Children under 8 yrs. can choke or suffocate on uninflated or broken balloons." | Age 8, not 3 |
| Battery toy | Battery safety and disposal statement | Plus compartment tool requirement where applicable |
| Button/coin cell | Prescribed ingestion warning | Required on product and packaging |

In the EU, warnings must be in the language of the country of sale, and the CE mark plus age warning must be on the product or packaging. Do not put the EU responsible person's details only in the listing — GPSR requires them on the product or packaging too.

Check the print size against the standard before the plates are made. A warning printed at 4 pt on a dark background fails review even when the words are right.

## Stage 4 — listing build

**12. Main image on pure white, product only.** No props, no logos, no text overlays. Toys attract lifestyle imagery, but the main image slot is not the place for it.

**13. Every accessory in the box is photographed.** "Set" listings with undisclosed pieces generate returns and authenticity complaints. If the box contains a charger, a strap or a spare wheel, show it.

**14. Attributes match the certificate.** Age range, material, battery type, number of pieces, and the model number must mirror the CPC or DoC. A mismatch between the listing and the document is treated as a compliance failure, not a data-entry error.

**15. Restricted-claims check.** Avoid "non-toxic", "100% safe", "FDA approved" and similar unless literally true and documented. Toy listings that claim absolute safety attract both complaints and regulatory attention.

**16. Packaging suffocation warning.** Polybags need the suffocation warning, and a bag above the size threshold needs the warning plus a hole or a thickness spec. Amazon's FBA prep rules also require it. See [packaging cost comparison](/blog/toy-packaging-cost-comparison/) for how the bag type interacts with cost.

**17. FBA prep for sets and multipacks.** "Sold as a set of 3" on the outer polybag, no combined barcodes, and each unit correctly labelled if it can be sold separately.

## Stage 5 — when Amazon asks for documents

A compliance review usually arrives as a case in the product compliance area of Seller Central, with a deadline. What clears it fastest:

- A **single PDF per document**, clearly named by ASIN and document type.
- The CPC or DoC on letterhead, signed, dated, with the importer's name and address.
- The full lab report behind it, not just the summary page.
- Product images showing the label and the packaged unit.
- If the document is older than a material change, say so and supply the new one — do not submit a report that silently predates a supplier change.

What reliably fails: a certificate for a different model, a report from a lab without CPSC acceptance, a document with the factory as importer when you are the importer, and a photo of a label that does not match the listing's age grade.

## Restricted and high-scrutiny items

Some toy types deserve a decision before sourcing rather than after:

| Item | Status to verify |
|---|---|
| High-powered magnet sets | Regulated in the US; strict marketplace restrictions |
| Water beads | Marketplace restrictions; ingestion hazard |
| Button-cell novelties | Child-resistant compartment and warnings required |
| Laser toys | Classification and label rules; some classes not sellable |
| Projectile toys | Energy limits and tip requirements |
| Chemistry sets | Age grading and hazard labelling |
| Toy guns / realistic replicas | Marketplace policy restrictions in many countries |

If a supplier offers a "hot" item with no documentation, that is the item most likely to be pulled from the catalogue three months later, taking your inventory with it.

## The one-page pre-launch checklist

- [ ] Category gated? Approval obtained or plan confirmed
- [ ] Product checked against CPSC and Safety Gate recalls
- [ ] Age grade fixed and consistent across test, label and listing
- [ ] Importer (US) and responsible person (EU) identified and printed
- [ ] CPC issued by the importer, referencing the model and lab acceptance number
- [ ] CPSIA tracking label on product and packaging
- [ ] EN 71 + EN 62115 + EMC/radio reports for the EU
- [ ] GPSR listing fields complete
- [ ] UN38.3 + MSDS + test summary if a battery is included
- [ ] Warning text exact, in the right language, at the right size
- [ ] Label photographed from a production unit
- [ ] Main image white background, product only
- [ ] All box contents photographed
- [ ] Attributes match the certificate
- [ ] Polybag suffocation warning and FBA set labelling
- [ ] Documents renamed and archived by ASIN
- [ ] Restricted-item review completed

## The short version

- The certificate is one item on a checklist, not the checklist.
- Tracking labels, warning wording, age grading and importer identity cause as many failures as the test report.
- The EU now enforces listing-level manufacturer and responsible-person fields; the US enforces tracking labels.
- Battery toys need transport documents as well as safety reports.
- Decide age grade, importer and responsible person before anything is printed.
