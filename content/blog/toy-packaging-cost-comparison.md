Two identical toys leave the same factory at the same unit cost and arrive in different markets at very different prices. The difference is usually packaging, and it is the line item buyers most often underestimate — both in cost and in lead time.

Packaging does three jobs: protects the product in transit, carries legally required information, and sells the product on a shelf. Which of those matters most determines what you should spend.

## Cost ranges by format

All figures are indicative for a mid-size toy (20–30 cm), based on Chinese packaging suppliers at 3,000+ piece runs.

| Format | Cost per unit | MOQ for custom print | Lead time | Retail channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polybag + header card | USD 0.03–0.08 | 1,000 | 5–7 days | Market stalls, online, dollar stores |
| Polybag + printed insert | USD 0.06–0.12 | 3,000 | 7–10 days | E-commerce, budget retail |
| Printed colour box (small) | USD 0.18–0.35 | 3,000 | 10–15 days | General retail, Amazon |
| Printed colour box (window) | USD 0.25–0.45 | 3,000 | 12–18 days | Gift retail, toy chains |
| Blister on printed card | USD 0.20–0.40 | 5,000 | 15–25 days | Supermarkets, drugstores |
| Rigid gift box | USD 0.60–1.50 | 1,000 | 15–20 days | Premium, gifting |
| Display carton (shelf-ready) | USD 0.15–0.30 | 5,000 | 15–20 days | Supermarkets, club stores |

Add roughly 30–50% to these figures if you are ordering under 1,000 pieces, because printing setups are amortised over fewer units.

## The hidden costs nobody quotes

**Artwork and plate setup.** A four-colour printed box needs plates and a print proof. Expect USD 150–400 one-off, plus 3–5 days for the first proof and one revision round. Design changes after plate-making mean new plates.

**Structural die-cut.** If the box is not a standard size, a die-cut tool costs USD 80–250. Standard sizes avoid this entirely, which is why factories push you toward their existing box dimensions.

**Inner packaging.** The outer box is visible; the inner packaging is what prevents damage. A PVC window box, a moulded pulp tray, or a foam insert adds USD 0.05–0.25 and is often omitted from the first quote.

**Amazon-specific requirements.** FNSKU labels, polybag suffocation warning, "this bag is not a toy" printing, and ISTA-6 transit testing if you ship to FBA in a case pack. Budget USD 0.02–0.05 per unit and an extra week.

**Carton marking.** Shipping marks, barcodes, and country-of-origin printing on the master carton are usually free at the factory, but not always. Confirm.

## Polybag: when it is the right answer

Polybag is not "cheap packaging". It is the correct format for several genuine cases:

- **First order / market test.** No print setup, no plate cost, MOQ as low as 300. You can validate sell-through before committing to retail packaging.
- **Amazon FBA.** Many sellers ship in plain polybags deliberately, because the retail box gets damaged in the FBA warehouse and returns the product.
- **B2B and promotional.** Corporate giveaways do not need shelf presence.

The constraint: polybag is effectively banned from most physical retail in the US and EU under shelf-ready packaging rules from the major chains. If your plan is retail distribution, polybag is a trial format, not a final one.

## Colour box: the default for retail

A printed colour box (folding carton) is the standard for anything sold in a store. It carries the brand, the legally required info, and the barcode.

**Where costs go wrong:**

- **Custom size.** A bespoke box with a die-cut tool adds USD 200+ and 5 days. A standard size costs nothing. Design within standard dimensions unless the product truly requires otherwise.
- **Window vs no window.** A die-cut window plus PVC film adds USD 0.06–0.12 and gives the buyer a look at the product. Worth it for shapes that photograph poorly; not worth it for anything sold primarily online.
- **Coating.** Matte lamination looks premium and costs USD 0.04–0.08. Gloss is cheaper. Neither is required.
- **Spot UV or foil.** Adds USD 0.08–0.20 and is almost never justified on a toy under USD 10 retail.

**Watch for the "same box, thinner board" swap.** A 350 gsm box and a 300 gsm box look identical in a proof and differ in the hand. Specify board weight in the PO.

## Blister and display carton

**Blister** (formed plastic on printed card) is the supermarket format. It shows the product fully, prevents tampering and is hard to counterfeit. Downsides: it needs a forming tool (USD 300–800), it is the least eco-friendly option in an era of plastic reduction commitments, and EU retailers are actively moving away from it. If your market is European retail, expect pressure to avoid blister entirely.

**Display carton** (shelf-ready tray) is the format for club stores and supermarkets. The carton itself goes on the shelf, so the master carton is the retail unit. This changes your carton spec: it must be printable, structurally sound, and often ISTA-tested. It also changes your order structure, because you are now buying display-ready quantities.

## A practical packaging sequence

1. **First order:** polybag with a printed header card. Validate the product.
2. **Second order, if it sells:** standard-size printed colour box. No custom die.
3. **Scaling, if you enter physical retail:** invest in a custom box structure and a shelf-ready display carton.

This sequence avoids the two most expensive mistakes: paying for retail packaging before you know the product sells, and shipping polybags into a retail channel that will reject them.

## What to put in the PO

```
Format: folding carton, standard size 250 x 180 x 80 mm
Board: 350 gsm grey board, matte lamination
Print: 4C + 0, Pantone match to brand PMS
Window: none
Barcode: EAN-13, supplied by buyer
Country of origin: printed on box and master carton
Master carton: 24 pcs, 5-ply, shipping marks per attached spec
Proof: digital proof approval required before plate-making
```

That level of detail is what separates a packaging order that arrives correct from one that arrives with the wrong barcode and a 15-day delay.
