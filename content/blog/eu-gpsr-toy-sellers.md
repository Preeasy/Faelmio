Since 13 December 2024, every toy placed on the EU market is covered by the General Product Safety Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/988 — usually shortened to GPSR. It replaced the 2001 General Product Safety Directive, and it changed something that catches Chinese suppliers and their customers repeatedly: the party liable for a product's safety must be established inside the EU, and that fact must appear on the product, the packaging and the online listing.

CE marking and an EN71 test report do not satisfy GPSR. They are separate obligations that now stack.

## What GPSR is, and what it is not

| | GPSR | Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | All consumer products | Toys specifically |
| Core concern | General safety, traceability, responsibility | Toy-specific safety, EN71 tests |
| Key output | Risk assessment, technical file, responsible person | Test reports, CE marking, DoC |
| Status | Applies from 13 Dec 2024 | Still in force; replacement regulation in the legislative pipeline |

A toy sold in the EU needs both. GPSR does not replace the toy-specific requirements; it adds a layer of traceability and accountability on top of them. For the toy-specific side, see [EN71, ASTM and GPSR compared](/blog/en71-astm-gpsr-toy-compliance/).

## The six obligations that matter to a seller

**1. Only safe products may be placed on the market.** This sounds obvious, but GPSR requires you to be able to demonstrate safety through a documented risk assessment — not just a test report. The assessment must consider foreseeable use, including by children and vulnerable users.

**2. An EU-established responsible person (RP).** A manufacturer outside the EU must designate a natural or legal person established in the EU. Their name, postal address and email must appear on the product or its packaging, and in the online listing. The RP must be able to produce the technical file on request and cooperate with market surveillance authorities.

**3. Traceability.** The product must carry identification that lets authorities trace the batch or serial number, plus the manufacturer's name and address. A generic "Made in China" is not sufficient.

**4. Technical documentation, kept for 10 years.** Includes the risk assessment, test reports, declarations of conformity, and manufacturing records.

**5. Online listing disclosures.** This is where most Amazon and Shopify sellers get caught. See the table below.

**6. Incident reporting and corrective action.** Serious incidents and recalls must be notified through the EU Safety Business Gateway. If a product is unsafe, you must withdraw or recall it.

## What your listing must show

GPSR's online sales provisions require the following to be visible *before* purchase:

| Required on the listing | Detail |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer name | Legal entity name |
| Manufacturer postal address | Full address, not a city only |
| Manufacturer email | A working contact |
| **RP name and address** | If the manufacturer is outside the EU |
| RP email | A working contact |
| Product identification | Type, batch or serial number |
| Product images | Clear, showing the product as sold |
| Warnings and safety info | In the language of the member state of sale |

Amazon has been enforcing this through its compliance portal: sellers are asked to upload RP details and may be blocked from listing without them. Marketplaces are treated as having their own obligations under GPSR, which is why they push the requirement onto sellers aggressively.

## Who can be the responsible person

The RP must be a physical or legal person established in the EU. Realistic options:

| Option | Suitable when | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| Your EU importer | You sell via a distributor who imports | Included in the relationship |
| An authorised representative service | You sell direct / via marketplace | EUR 200–600 per year, sometimes per SKU |
| An EU fulfilment service provider | You use an EU 3PL and they agree | Often bundled, but confirm in writing |
| Your own EU entity | You have or open an EU company | Company costs |

A Chinese factory or a Chinese sourcing agent cannot act as RP. If a supplier tells you they will be your responsible person, they are either misunderstanding the regulation or describing an arrangement that will not survive an authority's check. ## The five misunderstandings we see most

**"We have CE and EN71, so we are covered."**
CE marking plus test reports cover the toy-specific safety requirements. GPSR adds traceability, an RP and listing disclosures on top. Both are needed.

**"Our freight forwarder will be the responsible person."**
A forwarder moving goods through customs is not automatically an RP. They may offer the service, but it must be a written appointment with the obligations spelled out.

**"GPSR only affects large sellers."**
It applies to every product placed on the EU market, including a 50-piece test order sold on Etsy.

**"The importer of record handles it."**
If you sell direct to consumers from China via a marketplace, you may be the one placing the product on the market, and the obligation is yours. This is precisely the situation GPSR was designed to close.

**"A label with our company name is enough."**
The label must carry the manufacturer's details *and* the RP's details, plus batch identification. A brand logo is neither.

## A practical compliance checklist

For each toy SKU you sell into the EU:

```
[ ] Risk assessment document (foreseeable use, age group, hazards)
[ ] EN71-1/-2/-3 test reports from an accredited lab, current batch
[ ] EU Declaration of Conformity, signed
[ ] Technical file assembled, stored 10 years
[ ] Responsible person appointed, written agreement in place
[ ] Product label: manufacturer name, address, email, batch ID
[ ] Product label: RP name, address, email
[ ] Packaging warnings in the language of the sale country
[ ] Listing updated with all fields in the table above
[ ] Incident reporting process defined (Safety Business Gateway)
```

If several of these are missing on a product you already sell, the exposure is not theoretical. Market surveillance authorities can order a withdrawal, and non-compliance penalties vary by member state — some run into the millions of euros for repeated or serious breaches. Marketplaces, meanwhile, simply delist.

## Where this fits in the buying process

The practical sequence for a new toy order into the EU:

1. **Before tooling:** confirm the age grade and decide who will be the RP.
2. **Sample stage:** commission EN71 testing on the final materials and artwork.
3. **Before mass production:** complete the risk assessment and the DoC.
4. **Before shipping:** finalise the label artwork — it is printed, so mistakes are expensive.
5. **Before listing:** fill in every GPSR field on the marketplace.

Label artwork is the step where we see the most avoidable cost. Printing 5,000 cartons without the RP's address means either reprinting or stickering every unit. ## The short version

- GPSR has applied since 13 December 2024 and covers all toys sold in the EU.
- You need an EU-established responsible person, and their details must be on the product, packaging and listing.
- CE and EN71 are still required and are not a substitute.
- Technical files must be kept for 10 years.
- Marketplaces are enforcing this now, not eventually.
