A factory quotes 1,000 pieces. You need 300 to test the market. Most buyers respond by asking for a discount on a smaller order — which is the one approach that never works.

MOQ is not a negotiating stance. It is a cost calculation. Once you know what drives it, you can change the inputs instead of arguing about the output.

## Why the number is what it is

A toy factory's MOQ usually comes from one of four costs:

| Driver | Typical trigger |
|---|---|
| Raw material minimum | Fabric, vinyl or resin sold in fixed roll/batch sizes |
| Machine setup | Injection moulding, printing, sewing line changeover |
| Printing / packaging | Cartons and colour boxes have their own minimums |
| Quality control | AQL sampling needs a minimum lot to be statistically meaningful |

So when a factory says 1,000, ask which of the four is binding. The answer determines which lever to pull.

If it is **packaging**, the MOQ on the product may be far lower — factories often bundle the carton minimum into the unit MOQ out of habit.

## Four levers that work

**1. Standard colours and materials.** Custom Pantone matching on fabric usually forces a full roll purchase. Accepting the factory's existing stock colour can cut MOQ by half or more. Ask: "What colours are you already running this month?"

**2. Plain packaging for the trial order.** A white box with a sticker instead of a printed colour box removes the printing minimum entirely. Print the retail box from order two, once you know the product sells.

**3. Combine SKUs, not volumes.** Three designs at 300 each is often acceptable where one design at 900 is not, because the factory spreads setup across the run. This is the single most effective tactic for a first order.

**4. Pay the setup cost separately.** If the binding constraint is a mould or a print plate, offer to pay that cost as a one-off line item. A USD 400 setup fee to unlock a 300-piece order is often better than committing to 1,000 units of an unproven product. Confirm in writing who owns the tooling afterwards — in our terms the buyer does, once it is paid for (see [how to order](/how-to-order/)).

## What does not work

**"We'll order more next time."** Every buyer says this. Factories discount it to zero.

**Asking for the small order at the large-order price.** The price at 300 units is legitimately higher per unit. Expect 10–25% above the 1,000-piece price and treat that as the real cost of testing.

**Going straight to a smaller factory.** A factory with a 100-piece MOQ is usually a workshop, not a manufacturer — no export licence, no test reports, and a quality ceiling you will hit on order two.

**Negotiating on Alibaba chat only.** The person answering chat is a salesperson whose commission depends on order value. Ask for the production manager's view on setup cost; the answer is different.

## When to accept the higher MOQ

Sometimes 1,000 is correct. If the product is a proven category (push-pop fidgets, standard plush mascots), the per-unit saving at 1,000 versus 300 usually exceeds the cost of holding the extra stock — especially once you count the second freight booking you avoid.

Run the arithmetic: (unit price at 300 − unit price at 1,000) × 300 versus the carrying cost of 700 extra units. For fast-moving novelty items, the higher MOQ wins more often than buyers expect.

## A workable script for the first message

> We are evaluating this product for [market]. Our trial order is 300 pieces across three designs, with a reorder within 90 days if sell-through is good. Two questions: which of the material, mould, printing or packaging minimums is driving your 1,000-piece MOQ, and what would the unit price be at 300 with stock colours and plain packaging?

That message gets a useful answer because it gives the factory a reason to solve your problem rather than defend a number.

## After the trial order

The thing that actually lowers MOQ permanently is a track record. A factory that has shipped you twice, on time, with no quality claims, will take a 300-piece order without being asked — because the risk calculation has changed. This is why the first order is always the expensive one, on every route, whatever the MOQ says.
