Why Custom Plush Toy Quotes Differ by 3x

Three factories quote USD 2.40, USD 4.10 and USD 6.80 for the same plush design. Here is what is actually different in each quote, and how to compare them line by line.

  • The three most common quote traps

  • A practical next step

Send one plush design to five Chinese factories and you will get quotes that differ by a factor of three. It is the single most common thing that confuses first-time buyers, and the usual conclusion — “the expensive ones are ripping me off” — is wrong about half the time.

A plush toy quote is not one number. It is a stack of eight decisions, and each one moves the price. When you compare quotes, you are almost never comparing the same product.

What is actually inside a plush quote#

Cost elementTypical share of FOB priceWhat changes it
Fabric20–30%Short pile vs long pile vs printed fabric
Filling8–12%PP cotton density, weight per piece
Cutting & sewing25–35%Piece count, curve complexity, embroidery
Embroidery / printing10–25%Number of colours, area coverage, 3D vs flat
Labour assembly5–10%Attachment of eyes, noses, accessories
Packaging5–15%Polybag vs printed colour box
Testing3–8%EN71, ASTM, or both
Factory margin8–15%Order size, payment terms, relationship

The first quote you receive usually states only the FOB unit price. Ask for the breakdown above and the three quotes stop looking contradictory.

The five differences that cause most of the gap#

1. Fabric weight and pile length

Short-pile plush is roughly USD 2.5–4.0 per kg; long-pile or printed plush runs USD 5–8 per kg. A 30 cm bear needs about 0.35–0.5 kg of fabric. That alone can swing the unit price by USD 1.00–1.50 with no other change.

Ask for the fabric specification in grams per square metre, not “soft plush”.

2. Filling density

Cheap quotes use 120–150 g of PP cotton in a 30 cm bear. Proper density for retail is 180–250 g. The visible difference is that the cheap one sags after a month of hugging and photographs flat. This is worth roughly USD 0.30–0.50.

3. Embroidery vs printing vs appliqué

Flat embroidery with four colours is cheap. 3D puff embroidery, appliqué patches, or large printed panels are not. A design with a detailed face can add USD 0.60–1.20 versus a simple one. Factories sometimes quietly swap a specified 3D nose for a flat one to hit a target price.

4. Certification scope

A quote with EN71-1/-2/-3 plus ASTM F963-23 test reports on file is USD 0.15–0.40 per unit more expensive than one that says “we can pass the test” but has no report. The second quote is not cheaper — it is unpriced risk. Ask to see the actual report number and the lab name.

5. Whether the mould/pattern is amortised

Custom pattern-making for a new plush shape costs USD 150–400. Some factories hide this in a per-unit loading, others quote it separately. Over 1,000 pieces the hidden version looks cheaper per unit and is not.

How to compare quotes properly#

Do not compare the unit price. Send every factory the same specification sheet and require the same fields back:

  • Fabric: composition, grams per square metre, pile length
  • Filling: PP cotton weight per piece
  • Size: finished dimensions, tolerance
  • Embroidery: stitch count and colour count
  • Accessories: eye type (plastic safety eyes vs embroidered), hang tags, labels
  • Packaging: polybag, colour box, carton quantity
  • Testing: which standards, which lab
  • Price basis: EXW, FOB, or CIF — this alone can be a 15% difference
  • MOQ and lead time

A quote that will not answer these in writing is not a quote, it is a guess. See how to order for the specification template we use.

The three most common quote traps#

“Same as sample” at a low price. The sample was made in the sample room by a senior sewer. Production runs on a line with 40 sewers. Ask for the production unit price tied to a signed golden sample, and put “quality to match approved sample” in the PO.

Price excluding testing and freight. A factory quoting EXW with no testing looks 20–30% cheaper than a DDP quote. Neither is wrong; they are different products. Normalise everything to FOB before comparing.

Price that silently drops the inner box. Retail-ready packaging is often 8–12% of unit cost. A quote that assumes bulk polybags is not the same product. See packaging cost comparison.

When the cheap quote is genuinely fine#

Roughly one in three times, the cheapest quote is legitimate. It happens when:

  • The design is simple (round shape, minimal embroidery)
  • The factory already runs that fabric and those trims in volume
  • The order is placed in a low season slot
  • The factory is building a relationship and prices the first order tight

The way to tell the difference is not to argue about price. It is to request a pre-production sample, then an inspection before shipment. Our quality and compliance process includes AQL 2.5 inspection at three stages, which is what turns a cheap quote into a cheap product that actually ships.

A practical next step#

Take your design, write the specification sheet above, and send it to three factories with one line added:

Please quote FOB Ningbo, state fabric weight in g/m², filling weight per piece, and which test standards you will supply reports for.

Three replies that answer all of it will be comparable within 10%. Replies that dodge the questions tell you something more useful than a price.
